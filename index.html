01 Getting started

Round Trip lives entirely in this one page — no login, nothing to install. Bookmark its link and it'll be exactly as you left it next time, because everything you enter is saved to this browser as you go.

From an empty ledger you have two options: Add your first trade, or Load sample data if you want to poke around with realistic numbers first. Loading sample data is additive-safe to try, and you can clear it later from this menu (see §07).

One browser, one ledger. Your trades are stored locally rather than in an account, so opening this link on a different browser or device starts a fresh, empty journal. To move your history, export a CSV from one and import it into the other (§06).

02 Logging a trade

  1. Click + Add trade in the top right (or, from an empty ledger, Add your first trade).
  2. Fill in the date and symbol — symbols are automatically uppercased.
  3. Pick ▲ Long or ▼ Short. This only affects how P&L is calculated — it's not a prediction of whether the trade won.
  4. Enter your entry price, exit price, and quantity. Fractional quantity is fine for anything sized in fractions, like crypto.
  5. Fees are optional and get subtracted from the result — leave blank for zero.
  6. Add a strategy tag (e.g. "Breakout", "Mean Reversion") if you use one — this is what powers the edge-by-strategy chart. Start typing to see tags you've already used.
  7. Notes are optional freeform text — what you saw, what you'd do differently.
  8. Watch the Realized P&L line at the bottom update live as you type, then hit Save trade.

To fix a trade later, click the ✎ icon on its row in the trade log — the same form opens pre-filled. The ✕ icon deletes it, after a confirmation.

03 Reading the numbers

The strip at the top of the dashboard summarizes every trade you've logged, updating instantly as you add, edit, or delete:

Total P&L
The sum of every trade's profit and loss, fees included. This is the headline number.
Win rate
The share of trades that closed positive. It says nothing about size — a high win rate with small wins and rare large losses can still lose money overall, which is exactly what profit factor is for.
Profit factor
Gross profit ÷ gross loss. Above 1 means your winners outweigh your losers in total; below 1 means the opposite, regardless of win rate. Shows ∞ if you have no losing trades yet.
Avg win / Avg loss
The average size of a winning trade and a losing trade, shown separately so you can see whether you're letting winners run and cutting losers short — or the reverse.
Current streak
How many trades in a row, most recent first, closed the same way — e.g. "3W" or "2L". Resets the moment a breakeven or opposite result appears.

04 Reading the charts

Equity curve

Your cumulative P&L, trade by trade, in the order you took them. The fill shades green while you're above breakeven and red while you're underwater — so drawdown stretches are visible at a glance, not just the final number. Move your mouse over the line for the exact date, trade, and running total at any point.

Daily P&L

A 13-week calendar, one cell per day. Darker cells mean a bigger day, in either direction; empty cells mean no trades that day. Hover any cell for the date and that day's total.

Edge by strategy

Net P&L grouped by the tag you gave each trade, sorted best to worst. This is usually the most useful chart in the app — it's where you find out that the setup you feel best about isn't necessarily the one making money. Trades left untagged are grouped under "Untagged," which is itself worth noticing if it's a large bar.

05 Working the trade log

Every trade you've logged sits in the table at the bottom, newest first by default.

  • Sort by clicking any column header — click again to reverse the direction.
  • Search matches symbol and notes text.
  • Side, Strategy, From/To filters narrow the table to exactly what you're looking at — useful for isolating "how did my Breakout trades do in July," for instance.
  • Clear filters resets all of the above in one click.

Filters only affect the table — the stat strip and charts above always reflect your full history, so you can filter the log without losing sight of the big picture.

06 Import & export

Both live under the ⋯ menu next to Add trade, top right.

Exporting

Export CSV downloads your full trade history as a CSV file — a plain-text backup, or something to open in a spreadsheet. If your current view can't hand you a file directly, Round Trip falls back to showing the CSV as text you can select and copy instead, so you're never stuck.

Importing

Import CSV accepts either a pasted block of CSV text or an uploaded file, using this column layout:

date,symbol,side,entry,exit,qty,fees,tag,notes
2026-08-01,AAPL,long,190.10,193.40,20,1.00,Breakout,broke the July high on volume
ColumnNotes
dateRequired. Format YYYY-MM-DD.
symbol, sideRequired. Side accepts long/short, or buy/sell as aliases.
entry, exit, qtyRequired, numeric. Quantity must be greater than zero.
fees, tag, notesAll optional — omit the column entirely or leave cells blank.

Click Preview import first — it shows exactly which rows will import and flags any that will be skipped (missing fields, bad dates, that kind of thing) before anything is added. Nothing is written to your ledger until you click Import trades on that preview.

07 Your data

Round Trip doesn't have a server — every trade is written to this browser's local storage on this device, and nowhere else. That means:

  • Nobody but you can see it, including Claude.
  • Clearing your browser's site data, using a private/incognito window, or switching browsers or devices will not show your existing trades.
  • There's no automatic backup — exporting a CSV periodically is the closest thing to one.
Clear all data, in the ⋯ menu, permanently deletes every trade in this ledger after a confirmation. Export first if there's any chance you'll want the history back.

08 Getting more out of it

  • Tag consistently. The edge-by-strategy chart is only as useful as your tags are — reuse the same handful of names rather than inventing a new one per trade (the tag field autocompletes from what you've already used).
  • Log the same day you trade. Entry/exit prices and the "why" in notes are both easiest to get right immediately after, not from memory a week later.
  • Watch profit factor over win rate. It's a better single signal of whether a strategy is actually working.
  • Use the heatmap to spot patterns — a string of red days clustered around a particular week is worth asking what was different about it.
